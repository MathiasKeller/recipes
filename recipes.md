## Scrambled Eggs
Source: https://www.recipetineats.com/scrambled-eggs/ (RecipeTin Eats, Nagi Maehashi)  
Serves: 2 · Prep: 1 min · Cook: 1 min

**Ingredients**
- **4** eggs
- **2 tbsp** (30 ml) milk, any fat %, optional
- **¼ tsp** (1.25 ml (≈ 1.5 g)) salt
- **2 grinds** black pepper
- **1 tbsp** (15 g) butter, or margarine or oil

**Ingredients — For serving**
- **4 slices** bread, toasted and slathered with butter

**Method**
1. Use a non-stick or well-seasoned cast iron pan of about 25 cm / 10″ and a rubber spatula or flat-edged wooden spoon.
2. Whisk the eggs, milk, salt and pepper.
3. Melt the butter over medium heat (medium-low if your stove is strong), then pour in the egg.
4. Wait 5 seconds, then slowly push the spatula in long strokes back and forth and around the edges, so raw egg runs onto the pan. After about 30 seconds, start gently folding as well.
5. Cook 60 seconds in total, until you have a pile of soft, custardy curds and no raw egg leaking at the edges.
6. Take the pan off the heat while the eggs are still slightly underdone for your taste. Keep pushing and folding for 10 seconds more; the residual heat finishes them.
7. Slide onto the buttered toast, sprinkle with extra pepper and eat immediately.

**Notes**
- Milk is optional. Cream can replace it (same amount, or half).
- Use a smaller pan for fewer eggs: about 20 cm / 8″ for 2 eggs.
- Don't stir fast or in circles. Slow, gentle strokes give soft curds.

## Poached Eggs
Source: https://downshiftology.com/recipes/poached-eggs/ (Downshiftology, Lisa Bryan)  
Serves: 1 egg · Prep: 5 min · Cook: 3 min · Equipment: Small fine-mesh sieve

**Ingredients**
- **1** large egg
- **1–2 tbsp** (15–30 ml) vinegar, optional

**Method**
1. Bring a large pot of water to a boil, then reduce the heat to low. Meanwhile, crack the egg into a small fine-mesh sieve over a bowl and swirl until the thin, liquidy white has drained off.
2. Transfer the egg to a ramekin or small bowl.
3. Stir the vinegar into the water to create a vortex. Add the egg to the centre and cook for 3 minutes.
4. Lift out with a slotted spoon, dab on a paper towel and serve immediately.

**Notes**
- Use the freshest eggs possible and at least 10 cm / 4 inches of water for a round shape.
- Don't salt the water; it makes more wispy whites.
- To make ahead, put the cooked eggs in an ice bath and refrigerate. Reheat in a bowl of boiling water for 20–30 seconds.

Source: https://cooking.nytimes.com/recipes/1014757-socca (NYT Cooking)

Pan: 30 cm / 12-inch cast-iron skillet · Oven: 230 °C / 450 °F

**Ingredients**
- **1 cup** (240 ml (≈ 90 g)) chickpea flour
- **½ tsp** (2.5 ml (≈ 3 g)) salt
- **½ tsp** (2.5 ml) black pepper
- **1 cup** (240 ml) warm water
- **3 tbsp** (45 ml) olive oil, divided
- **½** medium onion, sliced
- **½ tbsp** (7.5 ml) fresh rosemary leaf, minced

**Method**
1. Put a heavy, preferably cast-iron, 30 cm / 12-inch skillet in the oven and preheat to 230 °C / 450 °F.
2. Sift the chickpea flour, salt and pepper into a large bowl. Add the minced rosemary.
3. Whisk in the warm water and 30 ml / 2 tbsp of the olive oil. Cover and rest at room temperature for at least 30 minutes, until it has the consistency of thick cream.
4. Stir the sliced onion into the batter.
5. Take the hot skillet out of the oven, add the remaining 15 ml / 1 tbsp olive oil and swirl to coat.
6. Pour in the batter and bake 12 to 15 minutes, until firm with set edges. The top may not be browned.
7. Set the pan 5–8 cm / a few inches below the grill (broiler) for 1 to 2 minutes, just long enough to brown it in spots.
8. Cut into wedges and serve hot with toppings of your choice.

## Seared Scallop Ceviche with Citrus
Source: https://hellskitchenrecipes.com/dinner-party-appetizers-ramsay/ (Hell's Kitchen Recipes)  
*One of six starters on the page.*  
Serves: 4

**Ingredients**
- **12** large dry sea scallops, side muscle removed
- **1 tbsp** (15 ml) olive oil, plus a pinch of salt
- **Juice of** 2 limes, 1 lemon and ½ orange
- **½** small red onion, finely diced
- **1** jalapeño, seeded and thinly sliced
- **1** ripe avocado, diced
- **2 tbsp** (30 ml) chopped cilantro (coriander leaves)

**Method**
1. Pat the scallops very dry and season them.
2. Heat a heavy skillet over high heat until a drop of water skitters and vanishes. Add the oil and lay in the scallops without crowding. Leave them for 90 seconds, until deep gold underneath.
3. Flip and cook 30–60 seconds more, then move to a plate.
4. Slice each scallop into three coins. Toss with the citrus juices, onion and jalapeño and leave for 5 minutes, no more.
5. Fold in the avocado and cilantro and serve.

**Notes**
- The centres stay rare, so buy scallops sold for raw use (sashimi or sushi grade).
- For pregnant, very young, older or immunocompromised guests, cook the scallops 2–3 minutes per side until opaque throughout, and spoon the citrus over just before serving.
- After about 10 minutes in citrus, the slices turn chalky.

## Steamed Pork Buns — Baozi 包子
Source: https://thewoksoflife.com/steamed-pork-buns-baozi/ (The Woks of Life, Judy)  
Makes: 20 buns · Prep: 3 hrs · Cook: 20 min

**Ingredients — Dough**
- **about 1½ tsp (1.6 tsp)** (5 g) active dry yeast, weight is best
- **2 tsp** (8 g) granulated sugar
- **1½ cups** (355 ml) lukewarm water
- **about 5 cups** (580 g) all-purpose flour, plus extra for kneading and rolling

**Ingredients — Filling**
- **1½ lb** (700 g) ground pork, or ground chicken or beef
- **3 tbsp** (45 ml) water
- **3 tbsp** (45 ml) vegetable oil
- **2 tbsp** (about 20 g) ginger, minced
- **1 large** (about 200 g) onion, minced
- **2 tbsp** (30 ml) Shaoxing wine, or other Chinese rice wine or dry sherry
- **1 tbsp** (15 ml) dark soy sauce
- **1 tbsp** (15 ml) sweet bean sauce (甜面酱), or hoisin sauce
- **2 tbsp** (30 ml) ground bean sauce
- **1 tbsp** (15 ml) oyster sauce
- **1 tsp** (4 g) sugar
- **½ tsp** (2.5 ml) white pepper
- **2 tsp** (10 ml) sesame oil
- **1½ tsp** (7.5 ml (≈ 4 g)) cornstarch, mixed with 15 ml / 1 tbsp water
- **3** scallions, finely chopped

**Method — Dough**
1. Dissolve the yeast and sugar completely in the lukewarm water. Wait 10–15 minutes until it foams.
2. Add the flour about 60 g / ½ cup at a time and knead for about 15 minutes, adjusting the last portion so the dough is soft but neither sticky nor firm. Form a smooth ball, cover with a damp towel and proof for 1 hour somewhere warm. Make the filling meanwhile.

**Method — Filling**
3. Stir the 45 ml / 3 tbsp water into the ground pork until absorbed.
4. Heat a wok or cast iron pan until lightly smoking. Add the oil, ginger and onion and cook over medium heat until the onion softens.
5. Add the pork, raise the heat and break up any chunks. Cook just until pale and opaque; no browning needed.
6. Add the wine, dark soy sauce, sweet bean sauce, ground bean sauce, oyster sauce, sugar, white pepper and sesame oil. Stir on high heat until combined. Taste and adjust.
7. Cook a couple more minutes to evaporate the liquid, then stir in the cornstarch slurry and let it bubble for 30 seconds to 1 minute. Turn off the heat and cool uncovered. Once cool, mix in the scallions.

**Method — Assemble**
8. Turn the proofed dough out onto a floured surface and knead for 2 minutes to remove air pockets. Divide into 20 equal pieces of about 48–50 g each.
9. Roll each piece from the edge toward the centre, without rolling over the centre, so the edge is about half as thick as the middle. Add filling.
10. Pleat around the edge like an accordion, about 10–15 folds, until sealed at the top. Or skip the pleating: pinch the dough closed tightly and set the bun seam-side down.
11. Set each bun on a small square of parchment on the steamer rack. Cover and proof for 15 minutes.

**Method — Steam**
12. Start with cold water and medium heat, and steam for 15 minutes.
13. Turn off the heat and leave the lid on for 5 minutes before opening, or the buns will collapse.

**Notes**
- Keep your hands dry when sealing; any liquid makes the buns hard to close.
- Boiling water must never touch the buns. Oil the steamer sides and use a porous surface, not a plate.
- Reheat refrigerated buns for 1–2 minutes in the microwave or 5 minutes in the steamer. Steam frozen buns for 8 minutes without thawing.

## Stuffed Grape Leaves — Warak enab (dolmades)
Source: https://amiraspantry.com/stuffed-grape-leaves-dolmades/ (Amira's Pantry, Amira)  
Makes: 60 · Prep: 1 hr · Cook: 1 hr 20 min · Total: 2 hr 20 min · Equipment: 4-quart (≈ 3.8 l) deep pot, colander, flat plate to weigh the leaves down

**Ingredients — Filling**
- **2 cups** (480 ml (≈ 400 g)) short-grain rice
- **1 medium** yellow onion, chopped
- **¼ cup** (60 ml) olive oil, divided; 1 tbsp of it is for blanching the leaves
- **3 tbsp** (45 ml) tomato paste
- **15 oz can** (425 g can) tomato sauce
- **1 cup** (240 ml) parsley, chopped
- **½ cup** (120 ml) fresh cilantro, chopped
- **⅓ cup** (80 ml) dill, chopped
- **1 tbsp** (15 ml) dried mint
- **½ tsp** (2.5 ml) ground black pepper
- **½ tsp** (2.5 ml) salt, or to taste
- **½ lb** (225 g) preserved grape leaves, about half a jar

**Ingredients — Cooking liquid**
- **2½ cups** (600 ml) vegetable stock or water, warm
- **1** vegetable bouillon cube
- **1½ tbsp** (22.5 ml) lemon juice
- **1 tsp** (5 ml) tomato paste
- **2 tbsp** (30 ml) olive oil, or ghee or butter

**Ingredients — For the pot**
- herb stems and grape leaf stems, kept from the prep
- tomato or onion slices, no quantity given

**Method — Soften the leaves**
1. Give the grape leaves a quick rinse in cold water. Bring about 1.5 l water with 15 ml / 1 tbsp of the olive oil to the boil over medium-high heat.
2. Split the leaves into 4 batches and boil each batch for 3–4 minutes, draining it in a colander before adding the next.
3. Leave the leaves to cool until you can handle them comfortably.

**Method — Filling**
4. Heat the rest of the olive oil in a large pot over medium-high heat and fry the onion until translucent. Stir in the tomato sauce and tomato paste.
5. Add the rice, spices and herbs and cook for just 2 minutes. Turn off the heat and let the filling cool.

**Method — Roll**
6. Cut the stems off the cooled leaves and keep them.
7. Lay a leaf smooth side down on a board and put about 7.5 ml / 1½ tsp filling at the wide end.
8. Fold in both sides and roll up snugly. Repeat with the remaining leaves and filling.

**Method — Cook**
9. Cover the bottom of a 3.8 l / 4-quart deep pot with the herb stems, tomato or onion slices and the grape leaf stems.
10. Pack the rolled leaves in tight rows on top, leaving no gaps.
11. Warm the stock in the microwave for 30 seconds, then stir in the tomato paste and lemon juice and dissolve the bouillon cube.
12. Pour the liquid over the leaves and set the pot over medium-high heat.
13. As soon as the liquid bubbles at the edges, lower the heat and lay a flat plate on top of the leaves.
14. Cover and simmer on low for 75–90 minutes until fully cooked, checking for doneness after 1 hour.
15. When done, heat the 30 ml / 2 tbsp olive oil until very hot, pour it over the leaves, cover and leave to rest for 10 minutes.

**Notes**
- Jarred leaves are tougher than fresh ones, so they need softening in boiling water first.
- Keep the herb stems and grape leaf stems: on the bottom of the pot they add flavour and tartness, and the tomato or onion slices stop the leaves scorching.
- Some cooks add up to 60 ml / ¼ cup lemon juice to the cooking liquid, to taste.
- Dried mint works better than fresh here.
- The plate on top keeps the rolls firm and in place while they cook. The hot oil poured over at the end soaks in and makes them more tender.
- Storage: keeps 5–6 days in an airtight container in the fridge. Reheat in the microwave or in a pot over medium-low heat with a splash of water.
- Make ahead: the filling keeps up to 3 days and rolled leaves up to 2 days. Freezing isn't recommended, as the rice tends to break.
- Traditionally served with tzatziki or tabouli; some serve them as a meal with lamb chops.
- Variations: add ground lamb or beef to the filling, or leave out or adjust the herbs.

## Butter Chicken — Murgh Makhani
Source: https://www.littlespicejar.com/finger-lickin-butter-chicken-murgh-makhani/ (Little Spice Jar, Marzia)  
Serves: 5 · Prep: 20 min · Cook: 20 min · Total: 1 hr, plus marinating · Equipment: Dutch oven, blender

**Ingredients — Chicken marinade**
- **1 lb** (450 g) boneless chicken breast, cut into 4 cm / 1½ inch cubes
- **2 tbsp** (30 ml) tandoori masala
- **1 tsp** (5 ml) ginger paste
- **1 tsp** (5 ml) garlic paste
- **½ cup** (120 ml (≈ 125 g)) yogurt
- **1 tbsp** (15 ml) oil, used when cooking the chicken

**Ingredients — Butter chicken sauce**
- **2 tbsp** (30 ml (≈ 25 g)) ghee (clarified butter)
- **1** large onion, thinly sliced
- **1½ tsp** (7.5 ml) ginger paste
- **1½ tsp** (7.5 ml) garlic paste
- **14.5 oz can** (411 g can) crushed tomatoes, a standard 400 g can works
- **1 tsp** (5 ml) chili powder, optional, see notes
- **1½ tsp** (7.5 ml) coriander powder
- **1½ tsp** (7.5 ml) cumin powder
- **½ cup** (120 ml) heavy whipping cream
- **½ tsp** (2.5 ml) garam masala
- **¼ tsp** (1.25 ml) dried fenugreek leaves, crushed between your fingers

**Method — Marinate**
1. Whisk the tandoori masala, ginger paste, garlic paste and yogurt until smooth. Add the chicken and marinate for at least 20 minutes, ideally 12–24 hours, covered in the fridge.

**Method — Sauce**
2. Heat the ghee in a Dutch oven over medium heat. Sauté the onions for 5–7 minutes until translucent; don't let them brown. Add the ginger and garlic paste and cook for 30 seconds, stirring.
3. Add the crushed tomatoes, chili powder, coriander and cumin and cook for 5 minutes. If it bubbles hard, add about 60 ml / ¼ cup water.
4. Blend until smooth, adding up to 60 ml / ¼ cup water if needed. Blend in two batches if necessary, holding the lid down with a towel.
5. Optional make-ahead: refrigerate the sauce for 24–48 hours or freeze for up to 3 months. Bring it back to room temperature before continuing.

**Method — Finish**
6. Heat the 15 ml / 1 tbsp oil in the Dutch oven over medium heat. Add the chicken, discarding excess marinade, and cook for 5–6 minutes, browning all sides.
7. Add the sauce and heat through. When it bubbles, add the cream and garam masala. When it returns to a simmer, add the crushed fenugreek leaves. Serve over basmati rice or with naan.

**Notes**
- Boneless, skinless chicken thighs work too.
- No ghee? Use 15 ml / 1 tbsp butter plus 15 ml / 1 tbsp oil.
- If you use tandoori paste instead of powder, use 30–60 ml / 2–4 tbsp.
- Chili powder is optional. Kashmiri chili powder is traditional; cayenne works, using 1.25–2.5 ml / ¼–½ tsp for a milder result.
- Take the marinated chicken out of the fridge 30 minutes before cooking so the yogurt doesn't curdle.

## Philly Cheesesteak
Source: https://natashaskitchen.com/philly-cheesesteak/ (Natasha's Kitchen, Natasha Kravchuk)  
Serves: 4 · Prep: 15 min · Cook: 13 min

**Ingredients**
- **1 lb** (450 g) ribeye steak, trimmed and thinly sliced
- **½ tsp** (2.5 ml (≈ 3 g)) sea salt, or to taste
- **½ tsp** (2.5 ml) black pepper, or to taste
- **1 large** sweet onion, diced
- **8 slices** mild provolone cheese, not aged provolone
- **4** hoagie rolls, sliced ¾ of the way through
- **2 tbsp** (30 ml (≈ 28 g)) unsalted butter, softened
- **1** garlic clove, pressed
- **2–4 tbsp** (30–60 ml) mayonnaise, or to taste
- **2 tbsp** (30 ml) oil for cooking, named in the method, not the ingredient list

**Method**
1. Slice the rolls ¾ of the way through with a serrated knife. Dice the onion and slice the beef very thinly. Freezing the steak for 20–30 minutes first makes slicing easier.
2. Mix the softened butter with the pressed garlic and spread it on the cut sides of the rolls. Toast them on a skillet or griddle over medium heat until golden, then set aside.
3. Add 15 ml / 1 tbsp oil and sauté the onions until soft. Salt lightly and cook until caramelized, then transfer to a bowl.
4. Raise to high heat, add 15 ml / 1 tbsp oil and spread the steak in an even layer. Brown for a couple of minutes undisturbed, flip, season with 2.5 ml / ½ tsp salt and 2.5 ml / ½ tsp pepper, and cook through. Stir in the onions.
5. Divide into 4 portions, top each with 2 slices of cheese, and turn off the heat so the cheese melts without overcooking the meat.
6. Spread a thin layer of mayo on the toasted side of each roll. Lay a roll over each portion and use a spatula to scoop the cheesy beef into the roll as you flip it. Serve warm.

**Notes**
- Add or substitute sliced bell peppers and/or mushrooms if you like.

## Butter-Poached Lobster Tail
Source: https://blackberrybabe.com/2019/12/04/perfect-butter-poached-lobster-tail/ (Blackberry Babe, Michelle Goth)  
Yield: 4 · Prep: 20 min · Cook: 6 min · Equipment: Instant-read thermometer

**Ingredients**
- **2 tbsp** (30 ml) water
- **1 lb** (450 g) salted butter
- **4 × 4–6 oz** (4 × 115–170 g) lobster tails
- **½** lemon, juiced, optional

**Method**
1. Prepare the tails: with kitchen shears, cut up the middle of the underside of each shell, then gently pry the meat out with your fingers. Rinse in cold water and pat dry.
2. Optional: thread each tail onto a wooden or metal skewer to stop it curling.
3. Heat the water in a large saucepan or Dutch oven over medium heat for about 1 minute.
4. Reduce to low, add the butter and whisk constantly; don't let it boil. Once it has emulsified into a creamy sauce, add the lemon juice or garlic if using.
5. Add the tails and cook over low heat, spooning butter over them every 30 seconds. Cook 6 minutes total, 3 per side, flipping halfway. Work in batches if needed.
6. Remove when they reach 60 °C / 140 °F on an instant-read thermometer. Serve warm.

**Notes**
- Unsalted butter works too; add a hefty pinch of salt.
- Cold-water lobster tails are preferred.
- Save the shells for stock.
- Optional additions to the butter: white wine, herbs, garlic, lemon, cream, chili flakes.

## Spaghetti Puttanesca — Spaghetti alla puttanesca
Source: https://www.ricettedalmondo.it/spaghetti-alla-puttanesca.html (RicetteDalMondo.it, Marianna Pascarella)  
Serves: 4 · Prep: 10 min · Cook: 20 min

**Ingredients**
- **380 g** spaghetti — *spaghetti*
- **4 tbsp** (60 ml) extra-virgin olive oil — *olio d'oliva extravergine*
- **100 g** black olives, pitted — *olive nere*
- **8** salted anchovy fillets — *filetti di acciughe sotto sale*
- **1 clove** garlic, halved — *aglio*
- **1 handful** salted capers, rinsed — *capperi sotto sale*
- **400 g** chopped tomatoes, in large chunks — *pomodori a pezzettoni*
- **100 g** tomato passata, optional — *passata di pomodoro*
- **1 handful** pine nuts — *pinoli*
- salt, to taste — *sale*
- **1 pinch** fresh chilli — *peperoncino fresco*

**Method**
1. Warm the oil in a large frying pan with the rinsed capers, anchovies, a pinch of chilli, the pitted olives, the pine nuts and the halved garlic clove. Let it all infuse for a couple of minutes.
2. Add the tomato chunks and the passata (optional), season with salt and simmer the sauce for about 15 minutes, adjusting the heat so it ends up slightly thickened.
3. Meanwhile, cook the spaghetti in plenty of salted water. Drain them very al dente straight into the pan with the sauce and toss over moderate heat for a few minutes to absorb the flavour.
4. Serve piping hot.

**Notes**
- The original Neapolitan version uses only olives and capers; the Roman version adds anchovies.
- The author adds pine nuts and a little passata for extra creaminess.
- Whole olives look nicer; if they aren't pitted, warn your guests.

- **1 tbsp** (8 g) all-purpose flour
- **¼ cup** (60 ml) water

**Ingredients — Assembly and toppings**
- **2 layers** tortilla chips, to taste
- sour cream, to taste
- pico de gallo, to taste
- sliced jalapeños, to taste

**Method**
1. Heat the oil in a large pan over medium-high heat. When hot, add the onion and tomato and cook for 2 minutes, until softened.
2. Add the beef along with the garlic salt, onion powder, cumin, chili powder, paprika, oregano, black pepper, cayenne and salt. Break the meat up as it cooks and brown it for about 7 minutes.
3. Taste and add more salt if needed. Push the meat to one side so the fat collects in the middle of the pan, sprinkle the flour into the fat, then stir everything back together.
4. Pour in the 60 ml / ¼ cup water, stir well and simmer for 2 minutes, until the liquid thickens and coats the meat. Take off the heat.
5. Spread a layer of tortilla chips on a platter and spoon over half the meat. Add a second layer of chips and the rest of the meat, then top with sour cream, pico de gallo and jalapeños.

## Sweet Egg Buns — Liu Sha Bao 流沙包, Wok and Kin version
Source: http://wokandkin.com/liu-sha-bao/ (Wok and Kin, Jeannette)  
*The source gives both metric and US cup measures.*  
Makes: 11 · Prep: 2 hrs · Cook: 30 min · Equipment: Steamer, 10 × 10 cm baking paper squares

**Ingredients — Dough**
- **3½ US cups** (430 g) self-raising flour
- **½ US cup** (75 g) caster sugar
- **1 tbsp** (15 ml) baking powder
- **1 tsp** (5 g) instant active yeast
- **0.8 US cup** (190 ml) milk, full cream recommended
- **0.1 US cup** (30 ml) water
- **¼ US cup** (60 ml) oil

**Ingredients — Filling**
- **4** salted egg yolks
- **0.44 US cup** (100 g) unsalted butter
- **⅓ US cup** (70 g) icing sugar
- **3 tbsp** (40 g) custard powder
- **4 tbsp** (60 g) milk powder
- **2 tbsp** (30 ml) evaporated milk

**Method — Filling**
1. Steam the salted egg yolks for 20 minutes until bright orange. Mash with the flat of a cleaver until you have fine crumbs or a paste.
2. Mix the yolks, butter, icing sugar, custard powder, milk powder and evaporated milk until well combined. Chill for 20 minutes to firm up.
3. Roll into balls about 3 cm / 1.2″ wide and keep them in the fridge while you make the dough.

**Method — Dough**
4. Sift the flour into a bowl and make a well. Put the yeast, caster sugar and baking powder in the well, then add the milk and water a little at a time, mixing as you go.
5. Work it into a dough and knead for 20 minutes.
6. Add the oil and knead for 10 minutes more, until smooth and waxy. Cover with a clean towel and rest for 30 minutes. Add water if it's too dry, or flour if it's too wet.
7. Knead the risen dough for 2 minutes, divide into about 11 portions and keep them covered.

**Method — Shape and steam**
8. Roll each portion flat, about 5 mm / 0.2″ thick and 12 cm / 4.7″ wide.
9. Place a filling ball in the centre and wrap the dough around it, leaving an opening at the top. Pinch the opening closed and twist to seal. Tear off any excess dough and press the seal flat.
10. Place sealed-side down on a baking paper square. Rest for 10 minutes.
11. Steam over medium heat for 10 minutes. Serve immediately.

**Notes**
- Don't overwork the dough; let it rise as soon as it comes together.
- Keep the wrapper on the thick side so it doesn't tear.
- Steam on medium heat throughout; higher heat makes the filling burst out.
- For a runnier custard, double the evaporated milk.
- Custard powder can be replaced with cornstarch.

## Torrijas — Spanish milk-soaked fried bread
Source: https://amimaneracocinando.com/receta-torrijas-paso-a-paso/ (Cocinando a mi manera, Carmen)  
Makes: 8–10 torrijas · Soak: at least 30 min

**Ingredients**
- Day-old bread with a firm crumb, brioche or candeal type; no quantity given, enough for 8–10 thick slices — *pan con buena miga, del día anterior*
- **750 ml** whole milk — *leche entera*
- **75 g** sugar — *azúcar*
- Orange peel — *cáscara de naranja*
- Lemon peel — *cáscara de limón*
- Cinnamon stick, optional — *canela en rama*
- **2** eggs, beaten — *huevos batidos*
- Sunflower or mild olive oil for frying — *aceite de girasol o de oliva suave*
- Sugar and cinnamon for coating — *azúcar y canela para rebozar*

**Method**
1. Avoid soft bread or sandwich loaf. Cut day-old, or even two-day-old, brioche or candeal bread into medium-thick slices.
2. Heat the milk with the sugar, orange peel and lemon peel (and the cinnamon stick if using) just until it starts to boil. Remove from the heat, cover and leave to infuse for a few minutes.
3. Let the milk cool before using it. This is key for texture and stops the bread falling apart.
4. Lay the slices in a wide dish without overlapping. Strain the cooled milk over them, removing the peels, and soak for at least 30 minutes.
5. Carefully lift out each slice and coat both sides well in the beaten egg.
6. Fry in plenty of hot oil over medium-high heat, in batches, until golden. Drain on paper towels.
7. While still warm, sprinkle with a mix of sugar and cinnamon.

## Spanish Cheesecake — Tarta de queso de La Viña
Source: https://www.tiaalia.com/2019/03/tarta-de-queso-de-la-vina-receta-con-y-sin-thermomix.html (Tía Alia, Carmen)  
Serves: 8 · Prep: 10 min · Bake: 40 min · Mould: 18 cm, at least 6 cm deep · Oven: 200 °C, top and bottom heat, no fan

**Ingredients**
- **570 g** cream cheese, spreadable, Philadelphia type — *queso crema*
- **4** eggs — *huevos*
- **230 g** sugar — *azúcar*
- **280 ml** whipping cream, minimum 35% fat — *nata líquida (mínimo 35% de grasa)*
- **1 cucharada (tbsp)** (15 ml (≈ 8 g)) wheat flour — *harina de trigo*

**Method — Without a Thermomix**
1. Weigh all the ingredients into a deep, wide bowl and beat until smooth. Have everything at room temperature and start with the cream cheese.
2. Line an 18 cm high-sided mould with baking paper that has been wetted and crumpled, letting the excess hang over the sides.
3. Pour in the mixture and bake on the lower part of the oven, preheated to 200 °C with top and bottom heat, for 40 minutes.
4. When it has set, turn off the oven and let it cool inside.
5. Refrigerate until serving, then unmould and slice.

**Method — With a Thermomix**
1. Put all the ingredients in the bowl and mix for 30 seconds at speed 5–6, using the blades (no butterfly).
2. Line an 18–20 cm springform mould with wetted, crumpled baking paper, leaving the excess overhanging.
3. Bake for 40 minutes on the lower part of the oven, preheated to 200 °C with top and bottom heat. For a darker top, raise it to 220 °C.
4. When it has set, open the oven door slightly and let it come to room temperature inside.
5. Transfer to the fridge. Unmould, slice and serve once cold.

**Notes**
- Use whipping cream (35% fat), not cooking cream.
- For a 23 cm mould, bake for about 50 minutes.
- It's best made the day before.
- Goes well with red berries, jam or coulis.

## La Chicha de Venezuela — Chicha criolla venezolana, a rice and milk drink
Source: https://www.laylita.com/recetas/chicha-criolla-venezolana/ (Laylita.com, recipe by Jani Díaz)  
*The page doesn't say what size cup (taza) it means; the metric values assume 240 ml.*  
Yield: 6 glasses, 1.2 l · Prep: 10 min · Cook: 1 hr

**Ingredients**
- **1 taza (cup)** (240 ml (≈ 190 g)) rice — *arroz*
- **6 tazas (cups)** (1.44 l) water — *agua*
- **1** cinnamon stick — *astilla de canela*
- **300 g can** sweetened condensed milk — *leche condensada*
- **3 tazas (cups)** (720 ml) whole milk — *leche completa*
- **1 taza (cup)** (240 ml (≈ 200 g)) sugar, optional, add gradually — *azúcar*
- **1 cucharada (tbsp)** (15 ml) vanilla extract — *esencia de vainilla*

**Ingredients — To garnish and serve**
- **a couple of cucharadas (tbsp)** (≈ 30 ml) sweetened condensed milk, reserved from the can
- Ground cinnamon, to taste — *canela en polvo*
- Crushed ice — *hielo picado*

**Method**
1. Rinse the rice in a large strainer under running water for a couple of minutes.
2. Put the rice in a large pot with the 1.44 l / 6 cups water and the cinnamon stick. Cook on low heat, half-covered, for about 1 hour.
3. When most of the water has evaporated and the rice is a pasty mass, turn off the heat and rest for 15 minutes.
4. Remove the cinnamon stick. Blend the rice, preferably in two batches, for a few minutes until completely smooth with no lumps.
5. Add the milk, condensed milk and vanilla. Taste and add sugar if needed. Blend for a couple more minutes.
6. To serve, fill glasses halfway with crushed ice and top up with chicha. Garnish with condensed milk and ground cinnamon.

**Notes**
- Use broken-grain rice (type B or C). Avoid parboiled or flavoured rice.
- Cook the rice well over 45 minutes so it's very soft; the softer it is, the creamier the chicha.
- Blend thoroughly. There's no need to strain it.

## Kaiserschmarrn — Kaiserschmarrn ganz klassisch
Source: https://emmikochteinfach.de/kaiserschmarrn-ganz-klassisch/ (Emmi kocht einfach, Emmi)  
Serves: 4 · Prep: 10 min · Cook: 10 min · Pan: 32 cm non-stick

**Ingredients**
- **4** eggs, size M, the size matters for the consistency — *Eier, Größe M*
- **300 ml** whole milk, 3.5% or 3.8% fat — *Vollmilch*
- **125 g** flour, type 405 (plain) — *Mehl, Type 405*
- **75 ml** sparkling mineral water — *Mineralwasser mit Kohlensäure*
- **60 g** sugar — *Zucker*
- **40 g** butter — *Butter*
- **1 pinch** salt — *Prise Salz*
- **1 Päckchen (sachet)** (≈ 8 g) Bourbon vanilla sugar — *Bourbon Vanillezucker*
- Icing sugar for dusting — *Puderzucker zum Bestäuben*

**Ingredients — Optional raisins**
- **40 g** dried raisins — *Rosinen, getrocknet*
- **2 EL (tbsp)** (30 ml) fruit juice for soaking, e.g. apple or orange — *Obstsaft zum Einlegen*

**Method**
1. If you're using raisins, soak them in the juice for about 15 minutes, or longer.
2. Separate the eggs. Beat the whites with a mixer until very stiff: you should be able to turn the bowl upside down without anything moving. Keep them in the fridge briefly.
3. Beat the yolks with the sugar, vanilla sugar and salt until creamy.
4. Stir in the flour and milk alternately, in portions. Finally stir in the sparkling water briefly.

7. Cut it into quarters with a spatula, flip them, and cook the other side for about 5 minutes until golden.
8. Tear the quarters into bite-size pieces, ideally with two spatulas, and cook a moment longer.
9. Dust with icing sugar and serve.

**Notes**
- With a 26 or 28 cm pan, cook the batter in two or three portions, otherwise it's too thick to cook through in time.
- Traditional accompaniments: apple sauce, Zwetschgenröster (plum compote), other fruit compotes, or vanilla sauce.

## Pumpkin Cupcakes — With cream cheese frosting
Source: Preppy Kitchen  
*Copied from text you supplied. The frosting quantities weren't included.*  
Bake: 20 min · Oven: 175 °C / 350 °F · Pan: Two 12-cup muffin pans, lined with paper cases

**Ingredients — Cupcakes**
- **2 cups** (240 g) all-purpose flour
- **½ cup** (100 g) granulated sugar
- **1 cup** (220 g) light brown sugar, packed
- **2 tsp** (10 ml) baking powder
- **1 tsp** (5 ml) baking soda
- **¾ tsp** (3.75 ml) salt
- **2 tsp** (10 ml) pumpkin pie spice, see notes for substitutes
- **2 tsp** (10 ml) ground ginger
- **1 tsp** (5 ml) ground cinnamon
- **15 oz can** (425 g can) pumpkin purée
- **1 cup** (240 ml) vegetable oil
- **4 large** eggs
- **1 tbsp** (15 ml) vanilla extract

**Ingredients — Cream cheese frosting**
- butter, softened; no quantity given
- cream cheese, no quantity given
- vanilla, no quantity given
- **1 pinch** salt
- powdered sugar, sifted; no quantity given

**Method — Cupcakes**
1. Heat the oven to 175 °C / 350 °F and line two 12-cup muffin pans with paper cases.
2. In a large bowl, sift together the flour, granulated sugar, baking powder, baking soda, salt, pumpkin pie spice, ginger and cinnamon.
3. In a second bowl, whisk the pumpkin purée, oil, brown sugar, eggs and vanilla.
4. Pour the wet mixture into the dry ingredients and stir only until just combined.
5. Divide the batter between the cases, filling each about three-quarters full (roughly 60 ml / ¼ cup each). Bake for 20 minutes, until a toothpick pushed into the centre comes out clean.
6. Leave the cupcakes in the pans for 10 minutes, then move them to a wire rack to cool completely.

**Method — Cream cheese frosting**
7. In a stand mixer, beat the butter on medium speed until creamy, then add the cream cheese and beat until smooth.
8. Mix in the vanilla and a pinch of salt. With the mixer on low, add the powdered sugar gradually until incorporated.
9. Turn the speed up to medium and beat for about 1 minute, until light and fluffy.
10. Pipe or spread the frosting onto the completely cooled cupcakes.

**Method — Frosting with a Bosch MUM4 stand mixer**
1. Press the release button and raise the swivel arm until it clicks into the horizontal overhead position and stays put.
2. Push the shaft of the wire whisk up into the drive socket under the arm until it snaps in; tug gently to check it is locked.
3. Set the bowl on the base, line up the notches and turn it to lock. Lower the arm until it clicks shut; the whisk should sit in the centre without scraping the sides.
4. Add the softened butter and cream cheese, and beat on speed 2–3 for 1–2 minutes, until completely smooth and lump-free.
5. Switch off, then add the sifted powdered sugar, vanilla and salt. Start on speed 1 to avoid a sugar cloud, then work up to speed 3–4 for 1 minute, until light and fluffy, glossy and holding soft peaks.

**Notes**
- The ingredient quantities for the cream cheese frosting (icing) are missing from the source text — only a pinch of salt is given. Butter, cream cheese, vanilla and powdered sugar still need amounts.
- Pumpkin pie spice is hard to find ready-mixed in Swiss supermarkets. To make it, mix 4 parts ground cinnamon (Zimt), 1 part ground ginger (Ingwer), 1 part ground nutmeg (Muskatnuss) and ½ part ground cloves (Nelken), plus ½ part ground allspice (Piment) if you like.
- Swiss shortcut for pumpkin pie spice: Lebkuchengewürz or Magenbrotgewürz from the Migros or Coop baking aisle. They also contain star anise and cardamom, so the flavour is more like a European spiced biscuit. Imported pumpkin pie spice is sold at American Food Avenue, BritShop and larger Globus food halls.
- American brown sugar is fine white sugar coated in molasses; Swiss Rohrzucker is coarse and dry and won't give the same texture. For a close match, rub 200 g Feinkristallzucker with 1 tbsp (≈ 15 g) dark molasses or sugar beet syrup (Goldsaft / Zuckerrübensirup, near the jams at Coop and Migros) until fully blended.
- Other brown sugar options: Vergeoise blonde or brune (larger Coop stores, French-speaking Switzerland), light muscovado (Coop Megastores, Manor Food, Alnatura, Claro), Coop Naturaplan Jacutinga sugar, or imported Domino or Tate & Lyle brown sugar from Globus, Manor or American Food Avenue.
